Quantum Theory, Orbitals & Electron Configuration

Explore how electrons occupy quantized energy levels, sublevels and orbitals. Use the live orbital-box model to investigate Aufbau, Hund, Pauli, chromium and copper.

1. Quantum theory and the modern atom

Electrons have wave-like and particle-like properties. Their energies are quantized, meaning only specific energy states are allowed. Unlike Bohr's fixed electron orbits, quantum mechanics describes an orbital as a region of high probability of finding an electron.

  • Planck: energy exchange occurs in discrete quanta.
  • Einstein: light consists of photons with energy E = hν.
  • de Broglie: particles, including electrons, have wave-like behavior.
  • Heisenberg: position and momentum cannot both be determined with unlimited precision.
  • Schrödinger: wave equations describe the electron's allowed states and probability distributions.

3. Three rules for electron configuration

Aufbau principleFill lower-energy orbitals before higher-energy orbitals.
Hund's ruleIn equal-energy orbitals, place one electron in each with parallel spins before pairing.
Pauli exclusion principleNo two electrons in an atom have the same four quantum numbers; an orbital holds at most two electrons of opposite spin.

Common filling sequence through krypton: 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p.

The usual filling sequence is a useful guide rather than an absolute energy ranking for every atom or ion.

5. Two essential exceptions: 3d series

Chromium (Cr, Z = 24): expected [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴; observed [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵. The five d orbitals are singly occupied.

Copper (Cu, Z = 29): expected [Ar] 4s² 3d⁹; observed [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰. The five d orbitals are completely filled.

In each case, the actual electron arrangement has lower total energy. Electron-electron interactions and exchange effects contribute to the relative stability of these configurations. A half-filled or full d sublevel is a useful pattern, not a universal explanation for all elements.

Ion formation: When transition metals form cations, electrons are removed from the 4s sublevel before 3d. Example: Fe = [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶; Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶.

6. IB HL connections and check for understanding

Quantized electron transitions produce line spectra: emitted or absorbed photon energy matches the difference between two allowed states. Large jumps in successive ionization energies indicate removal of an electron from a more strongly bound, lower principal energy level. Unpaired electrons can make an atom or ion paramagnetic.
